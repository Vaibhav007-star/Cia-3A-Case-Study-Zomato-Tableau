[FIELD: WHICH DATA-QUALITY ISSUES DID YOU HANDLE, AND HOW? (STEP 2/3)]
1. Missing Values: Imputed 9 missing cuisine records as 'Not Specified' and parsed primary cuisines from 1,825 multi-label combinations.
2. Zero-Cost Outliers: Identified 18 zero-cost records (9 in India) and imputed them using a two-stage hierarchical median based on City and Price Tier to avoid division-by-zero in budget formulas.
3. Unrated Cold-Start Listings: Handled 2,139 unrated records (26.9% of total with rating 0.0) by creating an explicit 'Is_Rated' boolean flag and 'Rating_Status' dimension. This preserved new merchant onboarding while preventing a downward bias on active rating benchmarks (true active mean: 3.28 vs biased mean: 2.67).
4. Power-Law Review Skew: Customer review votes ranged from 0 to 7,931 with a median of only 19. Applied logarithmic transformation [ln(1 + Votes)] to linearize engagement and eliminate the popularity monopoly of legacy mega-chains.
5. Invalid GPS Coordinates: Flagged 497 records located at (0.0, 0.0) Null Island using a boolean 'Has_Valid_Coordinates' flag to ensure map accuracy.
6. Service Fragmentation: Combined disconnected binary flags into a unified 4-class 'Service_Profile' (Both, Online Delivery Only, Table Booking Only, Dine-in Only).

Copy and paste this exact block:

1. COMPONENT FORMULAS (Standardized on 0 to 100 Utility Scale):
- Quality Score (S_rating): (Aggregate Rating / 5.0) * 100
- Popularity Score (S_engagement): [ln(1 + Votes) / ln(1 + 7931)] * 100 (Log-scaled to eliminate power-law bias)
- Budget Fit Score (S_affordability): 
  * If Cost <= Budget: 100 pts
  * If Budget < Cost <= 2 * Budget: 100 - 100 * [(Cost - Budget) / Budget] (Continuous linear penalty)
  * If Cost > 2 * Budget: 0 pts
- Service Utility (S_service): 20 (base dine-in) + 50 (if Online Delivery = 'Yes') + 30 (if Table Booking = 'Yes')

2. COMPOSITE RELEVANCE SCORE FORMULA:
Relevance Score = (w_quality * S_quality + w_popularity * S_popularity + w_budget * S_budget + w_service * S_service) / Total Weight

- Balanced (Default): Quality 40%, Popularity 25%, Budget 20%, Service 15%

- Budget Hunter (Value-First): Quality 25%, Popularity 15%, Budget 50%, Service 10%

Heading: The Dilemma of Choice Overload in Hyperlocal Food Search
Bullet Points:
* Massive Choice Friction: In high-density dining hubs like Delhi-NCR, users face 7,947 restaurant options, causing decision paralysis (Schwartz, 2004).
* The Popularity Trap: Default sorting by total review volume concentrates 70% of traffic on legacy chains (mean cost ₹1,470 vs ₹450 market median).
* Operational Mismatch: 70% of the top 10 most-reviewed venues offer dine-in only and cannot fulfill online home delivery.
* The 5-Star Fallacy: Sorting solely by rating elevates low-sample niche venues while ignoring diner budgets.
* Hidden Gem Invisibility: 194 elite restaurants (rating >= 4.0, 53.6% delivery readiness) are pushed to page 10+ due to lower review counts (< 200).
